## 1. LOGICAL REASONING (Core Puzzle Style)

**What it is:** Statement-based puzzles where you set up equations or logical steps from plain English. This is the e-con Systems "signature" style — see Q1, Q2 of your sample paper.

**Method:** Convert every sentence into an equation or condition. Don't try to solve it in your head — write variables down.

**Solved Example (from your paper):** Mother + baby + dog = 170 lbs. Mother = 100 + (baby + dog). Dog = baby − 60% of baby = 0.4×baby. Let baby = b, dog = 0.4b, mother = 100 + b + 0.4b = 100 + 1.4b. Total: (100+1.4b) + b + 0.4b = 170 → 100 + 2.8b = 170 → b = 25. **Baby weighs 25 lbs.**

### Practice Problems

1. A father is 4 times as old as his son. In 20 years, he will be twice as old. Find their current ages.
2. Three friends A, B, C have some marbles. A has twice as many as B, and B has 5 more than C. Together they have 45. Find each one's marbles.
3. A bag contains only 5-rupee and 10-rupee coins, totaling ₹150 with 20 coins. How many of each?
4. A number is such that half of it, added to 6, is one-third of the number added to 20. Find the number.
5. A tank is filled by pipe A in 6 hours. Pipe B can empty it in 9 hours. If both are opened together, how long to fill?
6. A man's age is 3 times his son's age. Five years ago it was 4 times. Find both ages.
7. Sum of two numbers is 84 and their difference is 12. Find the numbers.
8. A jar has red, blue, green balls in ratio 2:3:5, total 100 balls. Find the number of blue balls.
9. In a class, 60% are boys. If there are 20 more boys than girls, find total students.
10. A shopkeeper marks up a product by 40% and gives a 10% discount. Find his overall profit percentage.

## 2. VERBAL & NON-VERBAL REASONING

**What it is:** Two parts — Verbal (word-based: analogies, coding-decoding, blood relations, syllogisms, direction sense) and Non-verbal (pattern/series with shapes, mirror images, figure completion).

**Method for coding-decoding:** Find the letter-shift or position rule (e.g., each letter +2 in alphabet). **Method for blood relations:** Draw a simple family tree diagram, don't hold it in your head. **Method for series (non-verbal):** Track ONE changing feature at a time — rotation, shading, number of sides, position.

**Solved Example:** If in a code, CAT = 24-1-20 (position in alphabet), decode DOG. D=4, O=15, G=7 → **DOG = 4-15-7**

### Practice Problems

1. If FRIEND is coded as GSJFOE, decode the same logic for CANDY.
2. Pointing to a photo, a man says "She is the daughter of my grandfather's only son." How is she related to him?
3. In a certain code, "PAPER" is written as "5137412" and "PEN" as "53712". How is "PEEP" written?
4. Find the odd one out: Circle, Square, Triangle, Sphere.
5. A series of figures shows a shape rotating 90° clockwise each step and gaining one dot. Draw/describe the 5th figure.
6. Complete the analogy: Doctor : Hospital :: Teacher : ?
7. Five friends P,Q,R,S,T are sitting in a row. Q is to the right of P, S is between Q and R, T is at one end. Determine one possible seating order given P is at the other end.
8. If South-East becomes North, and this direction shift applies to all directions, what does West become?
9. Statement: All pens are pencils. All pencils are erasers. Conclusion: Are all pens erasers? (Syllogism logic)
10. Identify the mirror image logic: if the original word is "MASK", describe its mirror image orientation.

## 3. GEOMETRICAL PROBLEMS

**What it is:** Applying area, perimeter, angles, and coordinate geometry formulas to solve numeric puzzles quickly.

**Key formulas to memorize:**

- Area of triangle = ½ × base × height; Area of circle = πr²; Area of trapezium = ½(a+b)h
- Pythagoras: a² + b² = c²
- Angle sum of polygon = (n−2) × 180°

**Solved Example:** A ladder 10m long leans against a wall, its foot is 6m from the wall base. How high up the wall does it reach? By Pythagoras: height² = 10² − 6² = 100−36 = 64 → height = **8 m**

### Practice Problems

1. A rectangular field is 40m long and 30m wide. Find the length of its diagonal.
2. Find the area of a triangle with sides 13, 14, 15 (use Heron's formula).
3. A circular wire of radius 14 cm is bent into a rectangle of length 22 cm. Find the rectangle's width.
4. The angles of a triangle are in ratio 2:3:4. Find each angle.
5. A ladder just reaches a window 24m high; the ladder is 25m long. How far is the foot of the ladder from the wall?
6. Find the number of diagonals in a hexagon.
7. A square and a circle have the same perimeter. The side of the square is 22 cm. Find the circle's radius.
8. Two poles of height 6m and 11m stand 12m apart. Find the distance between their tops.
9. A cube has a volume of 125 cm³. Find its surface area.
10. Find the sum of interior angles of a polygon with 9 sides.

## 4. DATA INTERPRETATION

**What it is:** Reading a table, bar chart, or pie chart quickly and answering ratio/percentage/comparison questions.

**Method:** Don't compute every value — the question usually needs only one column/comparison. Read the question FIRST, then scan the chart for just that data.

**Solved Example:** Sales (in units): Jan-100, Feb-150, Mar-120, Apr-180. What % increase from Jan to Apr? Increase = 180−100 = 80. % increase = 80/100 × 100 = **80%**

### Practice Problems

1. A pie chart shows Company X spends: Salaries 40%, Rent 20%, Marketing 25%, Others 15%, on a total budget of ₹10,00,000. Find the amount spent on Marketing.
2. A bar chart shows student scores: 45, 60, 55, 70, 50 in 5 subjects (out of 100 each). Find the overall percentage.
3. Table shows a company's revenue for 4 quarters: Q1=50L, Q2=65L, Q3=70L, Q4=55L. Find the average quarterly revenue.
4. In a class of 200, a pie chart shows 30% play cricket, 25% play football, rest play other sports. How many play "other sports"?
5. A table shows population growth: 2020: 1000, 2021: 1100, 2022: 1210. Find the constant annual growth rate.
6. From a bar graph of monthly expenses (Rent 8000, Food 6000, Travel 3000, Misc 3000), find what % is spent on Food.
7. Two products A and B had sales of 200 and 250 units resp. last year; this year 240 and 300. Which product had a higher % growth?
8. A pie chart divides a budget into 4 equal parts. If total is ₹80,000, find each part's value.
9. Given exam pass percentages over 3 years: 70%, 75%, 90% — find the overall trend and average.
10. A table shows 5 employees' salaries: 20k, 25k, 22k, 30k, 18k. Find the median salary.

## 5. ALGEBRAIC PROBLEMS

**What it is:** Setting up and solving linear/quadratic equations from word problems — the backbone skill for most puzzles in this test.

**Method:** Always assign a variable to the UNKNOWN being asked, then translate sentence by sentence into an equation.

**Solved Example:** The sum of a number and its reciprocal is 26/5. Find the number. Let number = x. x + 1/x = 26/5 → 5x² − 26x + 5 = 0 → (5x−1)(x−5)=0 → x = 5 or 1/5

### Practice Problems

1. Twice a number decreased by 7 equals 15. Find the number.
2. The sum of three consecutive integers is 72. Find them.
3. A number increased by 20% equals 60. Find the original number.
4. Solve: 2x + 3y = 12, x − y = 1.
5. A father's age is 5 times his son's. After 6 years, he will be 3 times his son's age. Find both ages now.
6. If x² − 5x + 6 = 0, find the values of x.
7. The perimeter of a rectangle is 60 cm; length is 6 cm more than width. Find both dimensions.
8. A number consists of two digits whose sum is 9. If 27 is subtracted from the number, digits reverse. Find the number.
9. Solve for x: (x+3)/(x−2) = 5.
10. Cost of 3 pens and 2 pencils is ₹36; cost of 5 pens and 3 pencils is ₹58. Find the cost of one pen and one pencil.

## 6. MATHEMATICAL PROBLEM MODELLING

**What it is:** Translating a real-world scenario (like the "Merry-Go-Round" puzzle in your paper, Q2) into a solvable equation. This is the hardest e-con Systems style — you must MODEL the situation before solving.

**Method:** Define your unknown clearly, write down what each clue means as an equation, and simplify.

**Solved Example (adapted from your paper — Sammy's riders puzzle):** "One-third of kids ahead of me, plus three-quarters of kids behind me, equals total riders." Let total riders = n, and Sammy is one of them, so kids ahead + kids behind = n − 1. If ahead = a and behind = n−1−a, and it's known ahead = behind (typical version of this classic puzzle): n/3 × … → This puzzle needs the assumption "ahead = behind" for a clean solve. Trying n=range values: testing n=25 → ahead=behind=12, 1/3(12)+3/4(12)=4+9=13=n−1+1(includes Sammy)→ n=13. **(Model it, verify by substitution — that's the key technique, not memorizing a formula.)**

### Practice Problems

1. A jar of coins has only nickels and dimes; total value ₹4.20 with 10 more dimes than nickels. Find count of each.
2. A car and a bike start from the same point in opposite directions. Car speed 60 km/h, bike 40 km/h. After how long are they 300 km apart?
3. A tank has two inlet pipes and one outlet. Model the fill time if pipe A fills in 4 hrs, pipe B in 6 hrs, outlet empties in 12 hrs, all open together.
4. In a classroom, chairs are arranged in rows. If 2 more students sit per row, 3 fewer rows are needed for the same 96 students. Find original rows.
5. A man mixes two types of tea worth ₹40/kg and ₹60/kg to get 10 kg worth ₹52/kg. Find the amounts of each.
6. A number of coins can be arranged in a perfect square, but 16 remain when arranging into a square with 5 more coins per row. Model and find the total coins (state assumptions).
7. Two trains start from stations 300 km apart, moving toward each other at 50 and 70 km/h. When and where do they meet?
8. A boat travels 24 km upstream in 6 hrs and returns downstream in 4 hrs. Find speed of boat and stream.
9. A factory produces 100 units/day; a new machine increases output by 20% but 2 workers are reassigned reducing output by 5 units/day net. Model daily output after change.
10. A ladder problem: rope 100m is cut into 2 pieces; one used for a square, other for a circle so that total area is minimized. Set up the modelling equation (no need to solve calculus — describe the setup).

## 7. TIME, SPEED, DISTANCE

**What it is:** Classic relative motion, trains, boats/streams problems.

**Key formulas:**

- Speed = Distance / Time
- Relative speed (same direction) = S1 − S2; (opposite direction) = S1 + S2
- Boat: Downstream speed = boat + stream; Upstream = boat − stream

**Solved Example:** Two trains 100m and 150m long run in opposite directions at 40 km/h and 50 km/h. Time to cross each other? Relative speed = 90 km/h = 25 m/s. Total length = 250m. Time = 250/25 = **10 seconds**

### Practice Problems

1. A car covers 240 km in 4 hours. Find its speed.
2. A man walks at 5 km/h and reaches office 10 min late; at 6 km/h he reaches 5 min early. Find the distance.
3. Two cars start at the same point, one going north at 60 km/h, other east at 80 km/h. Find distance between them after 2 hours.
4. A train 120m long crosses a pole in 12 seconds. Find its speed in km/h.
5. A boat's speed in still water is 10 km/h; stream speed is 2 km/h. Find time to cover 24 km downstream.
6. Two trains of length 200m and 300m run on parallel tracks at 60 and 90 km/h in the same direction. Time to cross each other?
7. A person covers a distance in 5 hrs at 40 km/h. At what speed should he travel to cover it in 4 hours?
8. A cyclist covers 15 km in 45 minutes. Find speed in m/s.
9. A train tunnel puzzle (like your paper's Q4): a man is 1/4 through a tunnel; running back he escapes just in time, running forward he also just escapes. Find the ratio of train speed to man's speed.
10. A and B start walking towards each other from points 60 km apart at 6 km/h and 4 km/h respectively. When do they meet?

## 8. TIME & WORK

**What it is:** How long it takes people/machines to complete work together, alone, or with some leaving mid-task.

**Key idea:** If A does a job in 'x' days, A's 1-day work = 1/x. Add rates to combine.

**Solved Example:** A can do a job in 10 days, B in 15 days. Together, how long? Combined rate = 1/10 + 1/15 = 3/30+2/30 = 5/30 = 1/6 → **6 days**

### Practice Problems

1. A can finish a job in 12 days, B in 18 days. How long will they take together?
2. A does a work in 20 days. He works for 5 days and leaves. B finishes the rest in 15 days. In how many days can B alone do the whole work?
3. 15 men can complete a task in 20 days. How many men are needed to complete it in 12 days?
4. A, B, C can do a job in 10, 15, and 30 days respectively. Working together, how long will they take?
5. A pipe fills a tank in 6 hours; another empties it in 8 hours. If both are open, how long to fill the tank?
6. A can do a piece of work in 8 days and B in 10 days. They work together for 2 days, then A leaves. In how many more days will B finish?
7. 8 workers can build a wall in 15 days. After 5 days, 4 more workers join. Find total number of days to finish.
8. A is twice as efficient as B. Together they finish work in 12 days. Find the time each takes alone.
9. Two taps A and B fill a tank in 15 and 20 minutes. A third tap C can empty it in 25 minutes. If all three are open, find time to fill.
10. 6 men and 8 women can complete a job in 10 days; 3 men and 4 women take 25 days for half the job. Find the time 1 man alone takes.

## 9. PRACTICAL & ANALYTICAL PUZZLES (Brain Teasers)

**What it is:** THE e-con Systems specialty — see your sample paper's rope-burning puzzle (Q5) and factorial-digit puzzle (Q3). These need logical creativity, not formulas.

**Method:** These usually have a "trick" — think about extremes, symmetry, or non-obvious combinations (e.g., burning a rope from both ends halves its time regardless of uneven density).

**Solved Example (Rope-burning, adapted from your paper):** You need exactly 15 minutes using two 1-hour ropes (uneven burn rate).

- Light Rope 1 at BOTH ends and Rope 2 at ONE end simultaneously.
- Rope 1 burns out in 30 minutes (both ends meeting).
- At that 30-min mark, light Rope 2's other end too — it has 30 minutes of rope left, but burning from both ends now takes only 15 more minutes.
- Total = 30 + 15 = **45 min** — Note: for exactly 15 min, light Rope 1 at both ends and Rope 2 at one end at t=0; when Rope 1 finishes (30 min mark) light Rope 2's other end; it finishes in 15 more minutes → but you wanted the _cooking_ to be 15 min, so start cooking the egg exactly when Rope 1 finishes, and stop when Rope 2 (now lit both ends) finishes 15 min later. **Egg cooks for exactly 15 minutes.**

### Practice Problems

1. You have two candles that each burn irregularly but take exactly 1 hour to burn fully. How do you measure 45 minutes?
2. You have 8 identical-looking balls; one is heavier. Using a balance scale only twice, find the heavier ball.
3. A man has 3 boxes: one has only apples, one only oranges, one mixed — but all labels are wrong. By picking one fruit from one box, how can he correctly relabel all boxes?
4. You're in a room with 3 light switches outside, controlling 3 bulbs inside a closed room. You can enter the room only once. How do you determine which switch controls which bulb?
5. 100 prisoners and light bulb puzzle: describe a strategy for prisoners to determine, using a single switchable bulb, that all 100 have visited a central room (classic explanation, no need for full formal proof).
6. A farmer must cross a river with a wolf, a goat, and a cabbage, using a boat that carries only himself and one item at a time. How does he get all three across safely?
7. Two ropes burn unevenly but each takes exactly 30 minutes fully. How do you measure exactly 45 minutes using both ropes?
8. You have 5 pirates dividing 100 gold coins by a voting rule (majority needed, senior pirate proposes). What split does the most senior pirate propose to survive and maximize his share? (classic pirate puzzle logic)
9. A man has 2 jugs of capacity 3L and 5L (no markings). How does he measure exactly 4 litres?
10. You are given 12 identical-looking balls, one of which is either heavier or lighter (unknown which). Using a balance scale 3 times, how do you find the odd ball and whether it's heavier/lighter?

## 10. PROBABILITY

**What it is:** Chance-based reasoning — see Q6 of your sample paper (car-on-highway probability).

**Key idea:** P(event) = favorable outcomes / total outcomes. For "uniform distribution over time," scale probabilities proportionally to time interval, but remember P for "at least one occurrence" needs the complement rule: P(at least 1) = 1 − P(none).

**Solved Example (from your paper):** P(seeing a car in 30 min) = 0.875. Find P(seeing a car in 10 min), assuming uniform distribution. P(no car in 30 min) = 1 − 0.875 = 0.125 = (P no car in 10 min)³ → P(no car in 10 min) = 0.125^(1/3) = 0.5 So P(car in 10 min) = 1 − 0.5 = **0.5**

### Practice Problems

1. A die is rolled once. Find the probability of getting a number greater than 4.
2. Two coins are tossed. Find the probability of getting exactly one head.
3. A bag has 4 red and 6 blue balls. One ball is drawn at random. Find P(red).
4. From a deck of 52 cards, one card is drawn. Find the probability it's a king or a heart.
5. If P(rain today) = 0.3, find the probability it does NOT rain.
6. Two dice are rolled. Find the probability that the sum is 7.
7. A box has 3 defective and 7 working bulbs. Two bulbs are drawn without replacement. Find P(both defective).
8. If P(seeing a bus in 20 min) = 0.75, find P(seeing a bus in 5 min), assuming uniform distribution (same style as your paper's Q6).
9. Three coins are tossed together. Find P(at least 2 heads).
10. A card is drawn from a standard deck; find P(it is NOT a face card).

## 11. COMBINATORICS

**What it is:** Counting arrangements and selections — permutations (order matters) vs combinations (order doesn't).

**Key formulas:**

- Permutation: nPr = n!/(n−r)!
- Combination: nCr = n!/(r!(n−r)!)

**Solved Example:** In how many ways can 3 people be selected from a group of 8 for a team (order doesn't matter)? 8C3 = 8!/(3!5!) = **56 ways**

### Practice Problems

1. How many ways can the letters of the word "TABLE" be arranged?
2. In how many ways can 5 books be arranged on a shelf?
3. From a group of 10 people, how many ways can a committee of 4 be chosen?
4. How many 3-digit numbers can be formed using digits 1-9 without repetition?
5. In how many ways can 4 boys and 3 girls be seated in a row such that all girls sit together?
6. How many ways can you choose a president and vice-president from a group of 6 people (order matters)?
7. How many diagonals can be drawn in a polygon with 10 sides using combinations?
8. A password uses 4 digits (0-9), repetition allowed. How many different passwords are possible?
9. From 5 men and 4 women, form a committee of 3 men and 2 women. How many ways?
10. How many ways can 6 different colored balls be arranged in a circle? (circular permutation)

## 12. STATISTICS

**What it is:** Mean, median, mode, standard deviation-type reasoning applied to small data sets.

**Key formulas:**

- Mean = sum of values / number of values
- Median = middle value (sorted); for even count, average of two middle values
- Mode = most frequent value

**Solved Example:** Find mean, median, mode of: 4, 8, 6, 5, 3, 8, 9 Sorted: 3,4,5,6,8,8,9 → Median = 6 (middle of 7 values) Mean = (4+8+6+5+3+8+9)/7 = 43/7 ≈ 6.14 Mode = **8** (appears twice)

### Practice Problems

1. Find the mean of: 12, 15, 18, 20, 25.
2. Find the median of: 5, 9, 2, 8, 7, 3, 10.
3. Find the mode of: 2, 3, 4, 4, 5, 5, 5, 6.
4. The mean of 5 numbers is 20. If one number is removed, the mean becomes 18. Find the removed number.
5. Marks of 6 students: 70, 85, 90, 65, 75, 80. Find mean and median.
6. If the mean of 10 numbers is 15 and one number 25 is replaced by 35, find the new mean.
7. Find the range of the data set: 12, 45, 7, 89, 34, 23.
8. A data set has values 4, 4, 4, 4. Find its standard deviation (explain reasoning, no need for full formula computation).
9. The average age of a class of 30 students is 12 years. If the teacher's age (40) is included, find the new average.
10. Two sets have means 10 and 20 with 5 and 10 items respectively. Find the combined mean.

## 13. PHYSICS & PHYSICS PROPERTIES

**What it is:** Basic mechanics, units, and conceptual physics reasoning — common since e-con Systems hires many ECE/hardware-background students. Expect simple numeric or conceptual questions, not deep derivations.

**Key concepts:** Speed/velocity/acceleration, force = mass × acceleration, work = force × distance, basic circuit concepts (Ohm's law: V=IR), density = mass/volume, simple properties of materials (conductors vs insulators).

**Solved Example:** A 2 kg object accelerates at 3 m/s². Find the force applied. F = ma = 2 × 3 = **6 N**

### Practice Problems

1. A car accelerates from rest to 20 m/s in 5 seconds. Find its acceleration.
2. Calculate the force needed to accelerate a 10 kg mass at 2 m/s².
3. Find the density of an object with mass 50 g and volume 25 cm³.
4. A current of 2A flows through a resistor of 5Ω. Find the voltage across it (Ohm's Law).
5. A ball is dropped from a height of 20m. Find the time to hit the ground (use g=10 m/s²).
6. Define and give one example each of a conductor and an insulator.
7. Work done in moving a 5 kg object a distance of 10m against a force of 20N — find the work done.
8. Find the kinetic energy of a 4 kg object moving at 5 m/s.
9. A wire's resistance depends on its length, area, and resistivity — if length is doubled and area is halved, how does resistance change?
10. State Newton's three laws of motion in one line each, with a simple real-life example for each.
